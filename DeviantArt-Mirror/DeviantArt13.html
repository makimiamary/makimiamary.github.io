[SH4] Welcome to South Ashfield Chinatown

Decoding the Chinatown Signs in B12 of the Building World and Mapping the Area

[SH4] Welcome to South Ashfield Chinatown

Decoding the Chinatown Signs in B12 of the Building World and Mapping the Area

This is a mirror of the DeviantArt version linked above, provided as a backup and for easier access.

In one corner of the B12 area on the map in Silent Hill 4's the Building World, there is a small Chinatown. This time, I decoded several shop signs found there.

  1. 写真現像加工中心 (Photo Development and Retouching Lab)
  2. 来福 長寿 酒店 (Good Fortune, Long Life, Liquor Shop)
  3. 中華泰平飯店 (Chinese Taiping Restaurant)
  4. 粥 (Congee)
  5. 卯䏞火鍋亭 / Chinese BBQ (Hot pot & BBQ)
  6. 耶蕪治療中心 (A Quack Doctor's Chinese Clinic)
  7. 追椎 牛肉包 / Oisii Beef Ball（Buns & Balls)
  8. 海鮮酒家 (Seafood Tavern)
  9. 郭佛XX地産 (Chinese grocery store?)

1. 写真現像加工中心 (Photo Development and Retouching Lab)

This is the setting for "self-kidnapping," at a photo studio.

The sign says "写真現像加工中心 (Photo Development and Retouching Lab)" and also has "PHOTO STUDIO" written in English, so it seems to be a film developing shop. It feels kind of retro now.

These kanji are Japanese-made fake Chinese. In actual Chinese, it would be written as "照片冲洗中心."

On the right, there's a drawing of a camera and the word "美麗 (beautiful)" in red letters. I can't make out the red text above "PHOTO," though.

2. 来福 長寿 酒店 (Good Fortune, Long Life, Liquor Shop)

Right across from it, behind the crime-scene tape, is what appears to be a liquor shop called "来福 長寿 酒店 (Good Fortune, Long Life, Liquor Shop)."

The sign visible through the window behind Walter during the cutscene is from this location. Once you notice it, you can't help but look at the text, haha.

It's interesting that these locations are shared between the in-game data, cutscenes, and, as shown below, even the wallpapers and media assets. Because of this, it's clear that they were not just background scenery, but fully realized areas that had been carefully crafted in detail.

3. 中華泰平飯店 (Chinese Taiping Restaurant)

The shop across from there, behind Henry, is a Chinese restaurant called "中華泰平飯店 (Chinese Taiping Restaurant)."

Both are fake Chinese. Why is that?

Because when you turn the sign sideways, you can see the English words "LIQUOR STORE." In Japanese, "酒店" means "a store that sells alcohol," but in Chinese, "酒店" apparently means "hotel," so it doesn't fit the location at all.

Using "飯店" to mean a "restaurant" is also Japanese-style Chinese.

4. 粥 (Congee)

This shop is located between the Chinese restaurant and the hot pot restaurant. It's hard to see, but there's a sign above the shutter that says "粥 (congee)," suggesting this is a separate congee specialty shop.

The long horizontal menu sign is too blurry to read, but it appears they offer several different kinds of congee.

By the way, the old wallpaper shows this same spot from the opposite angle.

5. 卯䏞火鍋亭 (Chinese BBQ)

I haven't been able to identify the first two characters, "卯䏞," because they're difficult to make out on both the signboard and the neon sign. Since several other shop names in this area use Japanese puns written with Chinese characters, this could be another example. My guess is that "卯" is intended to be read as "u," making it a play on the Japanese word "umee (delicious)"?

Although "火鍋" normally translates to "hot pot," the neon sign below reads "Chinese BBQ," so this is probably a hot pot and BBQ restaurant.

6. 耶蕪 治療中心 (A Quack Doctor's Chinese Clinic)

The sign uses the characters "耶蕪," a pun on the Japanese word "yabu," which means a quack doctor.

There are actually a few more characters after "耶蕪" that look like "骨 (bone)皮 (skin) XX" followed by unreadable text. Those may be part of the clinic's full name, possibly indicating medical specialties such as orthopedics or dermatology. Either way, the intended joke is clearly that it's a quack doctor's clinic, haha.

From another angle, you can make out the characters "健康 (health)," though they're written in reverse order. The text below also looks like it might read "茶葉 (tea leaves)," again in reverse, though I'm not completely sure.

This sign is located in the clinic area, so it might be advertising health tea sold at a clinic. Or perhaps there's a tea shop on the second floor. If that's the case, would this make it the tenth shop?

And, this is the location of the media asset.

7. 追椎 牛肉包 (Oisii Beef Ball)

The left sign reads "追椎," with "oisii beef ball" written underneath, so I assume "追椎" is meant to represent the Japanese word "oishii (delicious)."

That said, "牛肉包" literally refers to beef buns, but the English text says "Beef Ball." The shop may simply sell both beef buns and beef balls."

8. 海鮮酒家 (Seafood Tavern)

The name literally means a place serving seafood and alcoholic drinks, so it's probably a seafood restaurant or a tavern.

There is a vertical neon sign for the restaurant, but the entrance is hidden behind the beef bun shop. It may have a recessed entrance or be located on the second floor.

9. 郭佛XX地産

I can make out the first two characters, "郭佛," and there is also a matching neon sign, but I can't read the rest. Like several of the other shop names, I suspect this is another Japanese pun written with Chinese characters. If anyone can decipher it, I'd love to know.

The neighboring sign contains two unreadable characters that look something like "X楽" or "X薬," followed by "地産."

Because of that, my best guess is that this might be a Chinese grocery store or produce shop, but I really can't tell what kind of business it's supposed to be.

Extra: Butter cake and cookie packages

While they're not items you can interact with, boxes of "Butter Cake" and "Cookie" are lying outside the Chinese restaurant "中華泰平飯店."



Similar boxes appear throughout the Silent Hill series, and they are easy to miss because the colors have faded into the surroundings.

🧭 Walking Tour Map 🧭

These are only a small portion of the areas in B12. I was amazed by how much detail went into places that are so easy to miss during normal gameplay. Honestly, it took taking screenshots and zooming in, playing with camera angles, tweaking the contrast, and looking at the textures before I could decode them.

The people who built all of this from scratch are absolute geniuses!
